§ 527

Selling or printing fake court forms

See penalty details Fraud & Money Title 13, Ch. 7 · Extortion

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Any person who shall sell or offer for sale, print, publish, or distribute any paper, document or written, typed or printed form, designed or calculated by its writing, typing or printing, or the arrangement thereof, to cause or lead any person to believe it to be, or that it will be used as an order or other process of a court when in fact such paper, document or written, typed or printed form is not to be used as the order or process of a court, is guilty of a , and each separate publication, printing, distribution, sale or offer to sell any such paper, document or written, typed or printed form shall constitute a separate offense, and upon conviction thereof in addition to any other sentence imposed the court may order that all such papers or documents or written, typed or printed forms in the possession or under the control of the person found guilty of such shall be delivered to such court or the clerk thereof for destruction.

History: Added by Stats. 1929, Ch. 593.

1 crime in this section

  1. Sells or offers for sale, prints, publishes, or distributes any paper, document or written, typed or printed form, designed or calculated to cause or lead any person to believe it to be, or that it will be used as an order or other process of a court

    Penalty
    • Guilty of a misdemeanor (no term is stated in this section)
    Other consequences
    • Each separate publication, printing, distribution, sale or offer to sell shall constitute a separate offense
    • In addition to any other sentence imposed, the court may order that all such papers, documents or forms in the possession or under the control of the person be delivered to the court or its clerk for destruction

Simplified explanation

Selling, printing, publishing, or distributing papers designed to look like court orders or process is a misdemeanor; each act is a separate offense, and the court may order the papers destroyed.
