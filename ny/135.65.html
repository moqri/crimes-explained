§ 135.65

Coercion in the first degree

Class D felony Violence & Threats Art. 135 · Kidnapping, Coercion and Related Offenses

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

A person is guilty of coercion in the first degree when he or she commits the crime of coercion in the third degree, and when:

1. He or she commits such crime by instilling in the victim a fear that he or she will cause to a person or cause damage to property; or

2. He or she thereby compels or induces the victim to:

(a) Commit or attempt to commit a ; or

(b) Cause or attempt to cause to a person; or

(c) Violate his or her duty as a public servant.

Coercion in the first degree is a .

2 crimes in this section

  1. 1 Commits such crime by instilling in the victim a fear that he or she will cause physical injury to a person or cause damage to property

    Conditions
    • Commits the crime of coercion in the third degree

  2. 2 Compels or induces the victim to commit or attempt to commit a felony, to cause or attempt to cause physical injury to a person, or to violate his or her duty as a public servant

    Conditions
    • Commits the crime of coercion in the third degree
    • Any of the following: the victim is compelled or induced to commit or attempt to commit a felony; to cause or attempt to cause physical injury to a person; or to violate his or her duty as a public servant
Applies to all 2 crimes above
Penalty
  • Class D felony (sentence set in Penal Law Article 70)

Simplified explanation

Coercion in the first degree is committing coercion in the third degree by instilling fear of injury or property damage, or by compelling the victim to commit a felony, cause injury, or violate public duty; it is a class D felony.
