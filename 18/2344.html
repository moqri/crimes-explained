§ 2344

Penalties

Up to 5 years Fraud & Money 1 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 114 · Trafficking in Contraband Cigarettes and Smokeless Tobacco

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) Whoever knowingly violates section 2342(a) of this title shall be or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

(b) Whoever knowingly violates any rule or regulation promulgated under section 2343(a) or 2346 of this title or violates section 2342(b) of this title shall be or imprisoned not more than three years, or both.

(c) Any contraband cigarettes or contraband smokeless tobacco involved in any violation of the provisions of this chapter shall be subject to seizure and forfeiture. The provisions of chapter 46 of title 18 relating to civil forfeitures shall extend to any seizure or civil forfeiture under this section. Any cigarettes or smokeless tobacco so seized and shall be either—

(1) destroyed and not resold; or

(2) used for undercover investigative operations for the detection and prosecution of crimes, and then destroyed and not resold.

Other consequences
  • Contraband cigarettes or contraband smokeless tobacco involved in any violation of this chapter are subject to seizure and forfeiture; the civil forfeiture provisions of chapter 46 apply, and forfeited items are destroyed and not resold, or used for undercover investigative operations and then destroyed and not resold

Simplified explanation

This section sets the criminal penalties for the federal contraband tobacco laws. It is a crime to knowingly deal in contraband cigarettes or smokeless tobacco, to knowingly break rules issued under these laws, or to make false statements in those records. Contraband tobacco can be seized and must be destroyed rather than resold.
