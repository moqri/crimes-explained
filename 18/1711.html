§ 1711

Misappropriation of postal funds

Up to 10 years Mail & Postal 9 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 83 · Postal Service

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, being a Postal Service officer or employee, loans, uses, pledges, hypothecates, or converts to his own use, or deposits in any bank, or exchanges for other funds or property, except as authorized by law, any money or property coming into his hands or under his control in any manner, in the execution or under color of his office, employment, or service, whether or not the same shall be the money or property of the United States; or fails or refuses to remit to or deposit in the Treasury of the United States or in a designated depository, or to account for or turn over to the proper officer or agent, any such money or property, when required to do so by law or the regulations of the Postal Service, or upon demand or order of the Postal Service, either directly or through a duly authorized officer or agent, is guilty of embezzlement; and every such person, as well as every other person advising or knowingly participating therein, shall be or in a sum equal to the amount or value of the money or property embezzled, whichever is greater, or imprisoned not more than ten years, or both; but if the amount or value thereof does not exceed $1,000, he shall be or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.

This section shall not prohibit any Postal Service officer or employee from depositing, under the direction of the Postal Service, in a national bank designated by the Secretary of the Treasury for that purpose, to his own credit as Postal Service officer or employee, any funds in his charge, nor prevent his negotiating drafts or other evidences of debt through such bank, or through United States disbursing officers, or otherwise, when instructed or required so to do by the Postal Service, for the purpose of remitting surplus funds from one post office to another.

3 crimes in this section

  1. Loans, uses, pledges, hypothecates, or converts to his own use, or deposits in any bank, or exchanges for other funds or property, any money or property coming into his hands or under his control in any manner, in the execution or under color of his office, employment, or service

    Ways to commit
    • loans · uses · pledges · hypothecates · converts to his own use · deposits in any bank · exchanges for other funds or property
    Who
    • A Postal Service officer or employee
    Exceptions
    • Except as authorized by law
    • Depositing, under the direction of the Postal Service, in a national bank designated by the Secretary of the Treasury, to his own credit as Postal Service officer or employee, any funds in his charge
    • Negotiating drafts or other evidences of debt through such bank, or through United States disbursing officers, or otherwise, when instructed or required so to do by the Postal Service, for the purpose of remitting surplus funds from one post office to another

  2. Fails or refuses to remit to or deposit in the Treasury of the United States or in a designated depository, or to account for or turn over to the proper officer or agent, any such money or property

    Ways to commit
    • fails · refuses
    Who
    • A Postal Service officer or employee
    Conditions
    • When required to do so by law or the regulations of the Postal Service, or upon demand or order of the Postal Service, either directly or through a duly authorized officer or agent

  3. Advises or participates in such embezzlement

    Ways to commit
    • advising · knowingly participating
    Mental state
    • Knowingly (as to participating)
Applies to all 3 crimes above
Federal basis
  • Money or property coming into the hands or control of a Postal Service officer or employee in the execution or under color of his office, employment, or service, whether or not it is money or property of the United States
Penalty
  • A fine under this title or in a sum equal to the amount or value of the money or property embezzled, whichever is greater, up to 10 years in prison, or both
  • If the amount or value does not exceed $1,000: A fine, up to 1 year in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime for a Postal Service worker to use, lend, or keep for themselves money or property handled on the job, or to fail to turn it over when required. This counts as embezzlement, and anyone who knowingly helps is also guilty.
