§ 1165

Hunting, trapping, or fishing on Indian land

Up to 3 months Animals & Wildlife None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 53 · Indians

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, without lawful authority or permission, and knowingly goes upon any land that belongs to any Indian or Indian tribe, band, or group and either are held by the United States in trust or are subject to a restriction against alienation imposed by the United States, or upon any lands of the United States that are reserved for Indian use, for the purpose of hunting, trapping, or fishing thereon, or for the removal of game, peltries, or fish therefrom, shall be or imprisoned not more than ninety days, or both, and all game, fish, and peltries in his possession shall be .

History: (Added Pub. L. 86–634, §2, July 12, 1960, 74 Stat. 469; amended Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(D), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2146 (PDF, 52 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Goes upon any land that belongs to any Indian or Indian tribe, band, or group, or upon any lands of the United States reserved for Indian use

    Federal basis
    • The Indian land is held by the United States in trust or is subject to a restriction against alienation imposed by the United States, or the land is land of the United States reserved for Indian use
    Mental state
    • Knowingly
    • Willfully
    • For the purpose of hunting, trapping, or fishing thereon, or for the removal of game, peltries, or fish therefrom
    Conditions
    • Without lawful authority or permission
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to 90 days in prison, or both
    Other consequences
    • All game, fish, and peltries in his possession shall be forfeited

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to go onto Indian land held in trust or restricted by the United States, or federal land reserved for Indian use, without permission in order to hunt, trap, or fish, or to remove game, furs, or fish.
