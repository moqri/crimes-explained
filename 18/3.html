§ 3

Accessory after the fact

See penalty details Courts & Justice 52 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 1 · General Provisions

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, knowing that an offense against the United States has been committed, receives, relieves, comforts or assists the offender in order to hinder or prevent his apprehension, trial or punishment, is an accessory after the fact.

Except as otherwise expressly provided by any Act of Congress, an accessory after the fact shall be imprisoned not more than one-half the maximum term of imprisonment or (notwithstanding section 3571) fined not more than one-half the maximum fine prescribed for the punishment of the principal, or both; or if the principal is punishable by life imprisonment or death, the accessory shall be imprisoned not more than 15 years.

1 crime in this section

  1. Receives, relieves, comforts, or assists the offender

    Ways to commit
    • receives · relieves · comforts · assists
    Federal basis
    • The underlying offense is an offense against the United States
    Knowledge
    • Knowing that an offense against the United States has been committed
    Intent
    • In order to hinder or prevent the offender's apprehension, trial, or punishment
    Exceptions
    • The penalty rule applies except as otherwise expressly provided by any Act of Congress
    Penalty
    • Up to half the maximum prison term and up to half the maximum fine prescribed for the main offense, or both
    • If the main offense is punishable by life imprisonment or death: Up to 15 years in prison

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to help someone you know has committed a federal crime avoid being caught, tried, or punished. A person who does this is called an "accessory after the fact."
