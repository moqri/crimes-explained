§ 2119

Motor vehicles

Death penalty possible Violence & Threats 264 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 103 · Robbery and Burglary

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, with the intent to cause death or serious bodily harm [1] takes a motor vehicle that has been transported, shipped, or received in from the person or presence of another by force and violence or by intimidation, or to do so, shall—

(1) be or imprisoned not more than 15 years, or both,

(2) if (as defined in section 1365 of this title, including any conduct that, if the conduct occurred in the , would violate section 2241 or 2242 of this title) results, be or imprisoned not more than 25 years, or both, and

(3) if death results, be or imprisoned for any number of years up to life, or both, or sentenced to death.

History: (Added Pub. L. 102–519, title I, §101(a), Oct. 25, 1992, 106 Stat. 3384 (PDF, 2.9 MB); amended Pub. L. 103–322, title VI, §60003(a)(14), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 1970 (PDF, 52 MB); Pub. L. 104–217, §2, Oct. 1, 1996, 110 Stat. 3020.)

1 crime in this section

  1. Takes a motor vehicle from the person or presence of another

    Federal basis
    • The motor vehicle has been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce
    Intent
    • With the intent to cause death or serious bodily harm
    Conditions
    • By force and violence or by intimidation
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to 15 years in prison, or both
    • If serious bodily injury (as defined in section 1365, including conduct that would violate section 2241 or 2242 if it occurred in the special maritime and territorial jurisdiction) results: A fine, up to 25 years in prison, or both
    • If death results: A fine, up to life in prison, or both, or death
    Key terms
    • "serious bodily injury": as defined in section 1365, including any conduct that, if in the special maritime and territorial jurisdiction of the United States, would violate section 2241 or 2242
