§ 2236

Searches without warrant

Up to 1 year Government & Corruption None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 109 · Searches and Seizures

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, being an officer, agent, or employee of the United States or any department or agency thereof, engaged in the enforcement of any law of the United States, searches any private dwelling used and occupied as such dwelling without a warrant directing such search, or and without reasonable cause searches any other building or property without a search warrant, shall be for a first offense; and, for a subsequent offense, shall be or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.

This section shall not apply to any person—

(a) serving a warrant of arrest; or

(b) arresting or attempting to arrest a person committing or attempting to commit an offense in his presence, or who has committed or is suspected on reasonable grounds of having committed a ; or

(c) making a search at the request or invitation or with the consent of the occupant of the premises.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 803 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 104–294, title VI, §601(a)(8), Oct. 11, 1996, 110 Stat. 3498; Pub. L. 107–273, div. B, title IV, §4002(d)(1)(C)(iii), Nov. 2, 2002, 116 Stat. 1809.)

2 crimes in this section

  1. Searches any private dwelling used and occupied as such dwelling

    Conditions
    • Without a warrant directing such search
    Exceptions
    • Does not apply to any person serving a warrant of arrest
    • Does not apply to any person arresting or attempting to arrest a person committing or attempting to commit an offense in his presence, or who has committed or is suspected on reasonable grounds of having committed a felony
    • Does not apply to any person making a search at the request or invitation or with the consent of the occupant of the premises

  2. Searches any other building or property

    Intent
    • Maliciously
    Conditions
    • Without reasonable cause
    • Without a search warrant
    Exceptions
    • Does not apply to any person serving a warrant of arrest
    • Does not apply to any person arresting or attempting to arrest a person committing or attempting to commit an offense in his presence, or who has committed or is suspected on reasonable grounds of having committed a felony
    • Does not apply to any person making a search at the request or invitation or with the consent of the occupant of the premises
Applies to all 2 crimes above
Who
  • An officer, agent, or employee of the United States or any department or agency thereof, engaged in the enforcement of any law of the United States
Federal basis
  • The offender is an officer, agent, or employee of the United States engaged in enforcing a law of the United States
Penalty
  • A fine (first offense)
  • If the offense is a subsequent offense: A fine, up to 1 year in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime for a federal law enforcement officer, agent, or employee to search someone's home without a warrant. It is also a crime for them to search other buildings or property without a warrant if done maliciously and without reasonable cause.
