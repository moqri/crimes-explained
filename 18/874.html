§ 874

Kickbacks from public works employees

Up to 5 years Government & Corruption None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 41 · Extortion and Threats

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, by force, intimidation, or threat of procuring dismissal from employment, or by any other manner whatsoever induces any person employed in the construction, prosecution, completion or repair of any public building, public work, or building or work financed in whole or in part by loans or grants from the United States, to give up any part of the compensation to which he is entitled under his contract of employment, shall be or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 740 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(K), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2147 (PDF, 52 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Induces any person employed in the construction, prosecution, completion or repair of any public building, public work, or building or work financed in whole or in part by loans or grants from the United States, to give up any part of the compensation to which he is entitled under his contract of employment

    Ways to commit
    • by force · by intimidation · by threat of procuring dismissal from employment · by any other manner whatsoever
    Federal basis
    • The employment is on a public building or public work, or on a building or work financed in whole or in part by loans or grants from the United States
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to 5 years in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to force or pressure workers on public buildings or projects paid for at least partly by federal loans or grants to give back part of their pay. This is called a kickback.
