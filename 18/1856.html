§ 1856

Fires left unattended and unextinguished

Up to 6 months Property Crimes None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 91 · Public Lands

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, having kindled or caused to be kindled, a fire in or near any forest, timber, or other inflammable material upon any lands owned, controlled or leased by, or under the partial, concurrent, or exclusive jurisdiction of the United States, including lands under contract for purchase or for the acquisition of which condemnation proceedings have been instituted, and including any Indian reservation or lands belonging to or occupied by any tribe or group of Indians under the authority of the United States, or any Indian allotment while the title to the same is held in trust by the United States, or while the same shall remain inalienable by the allottee without the consent of the United States, leaves said fire without totally extinguishing the same, or permits or suffers said fire to burn or spread beyond his control, or leaves or suffers said fire to burn unattended, shall be or imprisoned not more than six months, or both.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 788 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(G), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2147 (PDF, 52 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Leaves said fire without totally extinguishing the same, or permits or suffers said fire to burn or spread beyond his control, or leaves or suffers said fire to burn unattended

    Ways to commit
    • leaves the fire without totally extinguishing it · permits or suffers the fire to burn or spread beyond his control · leaves or suffers the fire to burn unattended
    Federal basis
    • Lands owned, controlled or leased by, or under the partial, concurrent, or exclusive jurisdiction of the United States (including lands under contract for purchase or condemnation), any Indian reservation or tribal lands under U.S. authority, or any Indian allotment while title is held in trust by the United States or remains inalienable without U.S. consent
    Conditions
    • The person kindled or caused to be kindled a fire in or near any forest, timber, or other inflammable material upon such lands
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to 6 months in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime for a person who starts a fire in or near a forest or other burnable material on federal or Indian lands to leave it without fully putting it out, let it spread out of control, or leave it burning unattended.
