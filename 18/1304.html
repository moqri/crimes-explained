§ 1304

Broadcasting lottery information

Up to 1 year Gambling & Obscenity None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 61 · Lotteries

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever broadcasts by means of any radio or television station for which a license is required by any law of the United States, or whoever, operating any such station, knowingly permits the broadcasting of, any advertisement of or information concerning any lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme, offering prizes dependent in whole or in part upon lot or chance, or any list of the prizes drawn or awarded by means of any such lottery, gift enterprise, or scheme, whether said list contains any part or all of such prizes, shall be or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.

Each day's broadcasting shall constitute a separate offense.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 763 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 100–625, §3(a)(4), Nov. 7, 1988, 102 Stat. 3206; Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(H), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2147 (PDF, 52 MB).)

2 crimes in this section

  1. Broadcasts any advertisement of or information concerning any lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme offering prizes dependent in whole or in part upon lot or chance, or any list of the prizes drawn or awarded by means of any such scheme

  2. Permits the broadcasting of any advertisement of or information concerning any lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme, or any list of the prizes drawn or awarded by means of any such scheme

    Who
    • A person operating any such station
    Mental state
    • Knowingly
Applies to all 2 crimes above
Federal basis
  • A radio or television station for which a license is required by any law of the United States
Penalty
  • A fine, up to 1 year in prison, or both; each day's broadcasting is a separate offense

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to broadcast ads or information about a lottery or game of chance on a licensed radio or TV station, or for a station operator to knowingly allow it.
