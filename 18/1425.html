§ 1425

Procurement of citizenship or naturalization unlawfully

Up to 25 years Immigration & Citizenship 10 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 69 · Nationality and Citizenship

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) Whoever knowingly procures or to procure, contrary to law, the naturalization of any person, or documentary or other evidence of naturalization or of citizenship; or

(b) Whoever, whether for himself or another person not entitled thereto, knowingly issues, procures or obtains or applies for or otherwise to procure or obtain naturalization, or citizenship, or a declaration of intention to become a citizen, or a certificate of arrival or any certificate or evidence of nationalization or citizenship, documentary or otherwise, or duplicates or copies of any of the foregoing—

Shall be or imprisoned not more than 25 years (if the offense was committed to facilitate an act of international terrorism (as defined in section 2331 of this title)), 20 years (if the offense was committed to facilitate a drug trafficking crime (as defined in section 929(a) of this title)), 10 years (in the case of the first or second such offense, if the offense was not committed to facilitate such an act of international terrorism or a drug trafficking crime), or 15 years (in the case of any other offense), or both.

Federal basis
  • Naturalization or citizenship evidence of the United States
Knowledge
  • Knowingly
Penalty
  • A fine, up to 10 years in prison, or both (first or second such offense, not committed to facilitate international terrorism or a drug trafficking crime)
  • If the offense is not a first or second such offense: A fine, up to 15 years in prison, or both
  • If the offense was committed to facilitate a drug trafficking crime (as defined in section 929(a)): A fine, up to 20 years in prison, or both
  • If the offense was committed to facilitate an act of international terrorism (as defined in section 2331): A fine, up to 25 years in prison, or both
Key terms
  • "International terrorism": as defined in section 2331 (violent acts or acts dangerous to human life violating U.S. or State criminal laws, appearing intended to intimidate or coerce a civilian population or influence or affect a government, occurring primarily outside the U.S. or transcending national boundaries)
  • "Drug trafficking crime": as defined in section 929(a) (any felony punishable under the Controlled Substances Act, the Controlled Substances Import and Export Act, or chapter 705 of title 46)
