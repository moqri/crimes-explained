§ 1263

Marks and labels on packages

Up to 1 year Transportation & Aviation None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 59 · Liquor Traffic

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever knowingly ships into any place within the United States any package containing any spirituous, vinous, malted, or other fermented liquor, or any compound containing any spirituous, vinous, malted, or other fermented liquor fit for use for beverage purposes, unless such shipment is accompanied by copy of a bill of lading, or other document showing the name of the consignee, the nature of its contents, and the quantity contained therein, shall be or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 761 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 90–518, §1, Sept. 26, 1968, 82 Stat. 872; Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(H), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2147 (PDF, 52 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Ships into any place within the United States any package containing any spirituous, vinous, malted, or other fermented liquor, or any compound containing any such liquor fit for use for beverage purposes

    Federal basis
    • Shipment into any place within the United States
    Mental state
    • Knowingly
    Exceptions
    • Unless the shipment is accompanied by copy of a bill of lading, or other document showing the name of the consignee, the nature of its contents, and the quantity contained therein
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to 1 year in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to knowingly ship a package of alcoholic drinks within the United States without a bill of lading or other paper showing who it is going to, what is inside, and how much.
