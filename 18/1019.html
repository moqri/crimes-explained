§ 1019

Certificates by consular officers

Up to 3 years Government & Corruption None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 47 · Fraud and False Statements

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, being a consul, or vice consul, or other person employed in the consular service of the United States, knowingly certifies falsely to any invoice, or other paper, to which his certificate is authorized or required by law, shall be or imprisoned not more than three years, or both.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 753 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(L), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2147 (PDF, 52 MB).)

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime for a U.S. consul, vice consul, or other consular employee to knowingly certify an invoice or other official paper falsely.
