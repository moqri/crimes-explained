§ 2279

Boarding vessels before arrival

Up to 6 months Transportation & Aviation None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 111 · Shipping

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, not being in the United States service, and not being duly authorized by law for the purpose, goes on board any vessel about to arrive at the place of her destination, before her actual arrival, and before she has been completely moored, shall be or imprisoned not more than six months, or both.

The master of such vessel may take any such person into custody, and deliver him up forthwith to any law enforcement officer, to be by him taken before any committing magistrate, to be dealt with according to law.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 805 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 103–322, title XXXIII, §330016(1)(D), Sept. 13, 1994, 108 Stat. 2146 (PDF, 52 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Goes on board any vessel about to arrive at the place of her destination

    Who
    • A person not in the United States service and not duly authorized by law for the purpose
    Conditions
    • Before her actual arrival
    • Before she has been completely moored
    Penalty
    • A fine, up to six months in prison, or both
    Other consequences
    • The master of the vessel may take the person into custody and deliver him to any law enforcement officer to be taken before a committing magistrate

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to board a ship that is about to arrive at its destination before it has arrived and been fully tied up, unless the person is in U.S. government service or authorized by law. The ship's captain may hold the person and turn them over to police.
