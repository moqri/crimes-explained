§ 111

Assaulting, resisting, or impeding certain officers or employees

Up to 20 years Violence & Threats 367 sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 7 · Assault

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) In General. Whoever—

(1) forcibly assaults, resists, opposes, impedes, intimidates, or interferes with any person designated in section 1114 of this title while engaged in or on account of the performance of official duties; or

(2) forcibly assaults or intimidates any person who formerly served as a person designated in section 1114 on account of the performance of official duties during such person's term of service,

shall, where the acts in violation of this section constitute only simple assault, be or imprisoned not more than one year, or both, and where such acts involve physical contact with the victim of that assault or the intent to commit another , be or imprisoned not more than 8 years, or both.

(b) Enhanced Penalty. Whoever, in the commission of any acts described in subsection (a), uses a deadly or dangerous weapon (including a weapon intended to cause death or danger but that fails to do so by reason of a defective component) or inflicts , shall be or imprisoned not more than 20 years, or both.

(c) Extraterritorial Jurisdiction. There is extraterritorial jurisdiction over the conduct prohibited by this section.

Applies to all 2 crimes above
Penalty
  • A fine, up to 1 year in prison, or both (where the acts constitute only simple assault)
  • If the acts involve physical contact with the victim of that assault or the intent to commit another felony: A fine, up to 8 years in prison, or both
  • If in the commission of the acts the person uses a deadly or dangerous weapon (including a weapon intended to cause death or danger but that fails to do so by reason of a defective component) or inflicts bodily injury: A fine, up to 20 years in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime to forcibly assault, resist, oppose, impede, intimidate, or interfere with a federal officer or employee covered by section 1114 while they are on duty or because of their work. It is also a crime to forcibly assault or intimidate a former federal officer or employee because of their past official duties.
