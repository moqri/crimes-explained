§ 403

Protection of the privacy of child victims and child witnesses

Up to 1 year Courts & Justice None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 21 · Contempts

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

A knowing or intentional violation of the privacy protection accorded by section 3509 of this title is a criminal contempt punishable by not more than one year's imprisonment, or a , or both.

History: (Added Pub. L. 101–647, title II, §225(b)(1), Nov. 29, 1990, 104 Stat. 4805 (PDF, 27 MB).)

1 crime in this section

  1. Violates the privacy protection accorded by section 3509 of this title

    Knowledge
    • Knowing or intentional (either suffices)
    Penalty
    • Criminal contempt punishable by up to 1 year in prison, a fine, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a crime (criminal contempt) to knowingly or intentionally break the privacy protections that federal law (section 3509) gives to child victims and child witnesses in court cases.
