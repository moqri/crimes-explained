§ 2192

Incitation of seamen to revolt or mutiny

Up to 5 years Transportation & Aviation None sentenced (FY2025) Ch. 107 · Seamen and Stowaways

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever, being of the crew of a vessel of the United States, on the high seas, or on any other waters within the admiralty and maritime jurisdiction of the United States, endeavors to make a revolt or mutiny on board such vessel, or combines, , or confederates with any other person on board to make such revolt or mutiny, or solicits, incites, or stirs up any other of the crew to disobey or resist the lawful orders of the master or other officer of such vessel, or to refuse or neglect his proper duty on board thereof, or to betray his proper trust, or assembles with others in a tumultuous and mutinous manner, or makes a riot on board thereof, or unlawfully confines the master or other commanding officer thereof, shall be or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

History: (June 25, 1948, ch. 645, 62 Stat. 800 (PDF, 39 MB); Pub. L. 104–294, title VI, §601(a)(8), Oct. 11, 1996, 110 Stat. 3498.)

Who
  • A member of the crew of a vessel of the United States
Federal basis
  • On the high seas, or on any other waters within the admiralty and maritime jurisdiction of the United States; a vessel of the United States
Penalty
  • A fine, up to 5 years in prison, or both

Simplified explanation

It is a federal crime for a crew member of a U.S. ship to try to start a revolt or mutiny, conspire to do so, urge other crew to disobey lawful orders or neglect their duty, gather with others in a mutinous way, riot on board, or unlawfully confine the captain or commanding officer.
