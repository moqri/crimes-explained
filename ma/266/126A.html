c. 266, § 126A

Defacement of real or personal property; penalties

Up to 3 years Property Crimes Ch. 266 · Crimes Against Property

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever intentionally, and or wantonly, paints, marks, scratches, etches or otherwise marks, injures, mars, defaces or destroys the real or personal property of another including but not limited to a wall, fence, building, sign, rock, monument, gravestone or tablet, shall be punished by imprisonment in a state prison for a term of not more than three years or by imprisonment in a house of correction for not more than two years or by a fine of not more than fifteen hundred dollars or not more than three times the value of the property so marked, injured, marred, defaced or destroyed, whichever is greater, or both imprisonment and fine, and shall also be required to pay for the removal or obliteration of such painting, marking, scratching or etching, or to remove or obliterate such painting, marking, scratching or etching; provided, however, that when a fine is levied pursuant to the value of the property marked, injured, marred, defaced or destroyed or when the cost of removal or obliteration is assessed, the court shall, after conviction, conduct an evidentiary hearing to ascertain the value of the property so marked, injured, marred, defaced or destroyed or to ascertain the cost of the removal or obliteration; and provided, however, that if the property marked, injured, marred, defaced or destroyed is a war or veterans' memorial, monument or gravestone, the fine under this section shall be doubled and the person convicted shall be ordered to perform not less than 500 hours of court-approved community service. A police officer may arrest any person for commission of the offenses prohibited by this section without a warrant if said police officer has probable cause to believe that said person has committed the offenses prohibited by this section.

1 crime in this section

  1. Paints, marks, scratches, etches or otherwise marks, injures, mars, defaces or destroys the real or personal property of another

    Ways to commit
    • paints · marks · scratches · etches · injures · mars · defaces · destroys
    Intent
    • Intentionally, willfully and maliciously or wantonly
    Penalty
    • Up to 3 years in a state prison, or up to 2 years in a house of correction, or a fine of up to $1,500 or up to three times the value of the property, whichever is greater, or both imprisonment and fine
    • If the property is a war or veterans' memorial, monument or gravestone: The fine is doubled and not less than 500 hours of court-approved community service is orderedmandatory minimum
    Other consequences
    • Must pay for the removal or obliteration of the painting, marking, scratching or etching, or remove or obliterate it
    • Court conducts an evidentiary hearing after conviction on the value of the property or the cost of removal when a fine is based on value or removal cost is assessed
    • If the property is a war or veterans' memorial, monument or gravestone, the fine is doubled and the person must perform not less than 500 hours of court-approved community service
    • A police officer may arrest without a warrant with probable cause

Simplified explanation

Intentionally, willfully and maliciously (or wantonly) defacing or destroying another's property is punishable by up to 3 years in state prison or 2 years in a house of correction, a fine, and paying for cleanup.
