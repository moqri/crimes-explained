c. 266, § 28

Motor vehicle or trailer; theft or concealment; operation without owner's consent after revocation of license; penalty

Up to 15 years Property Crimes Ch. 266 · Crimes Against Property

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) Whoever steals a motor vehicle or trailer, whoever damages a motor vehicle or trailer, whoever buys, receives, possesses, conceals, or obtains control of a motor vehicle or trailer, knowing or the same to have been stolen, or whoever takes a motor vehicle without the authority of the owner and steals from it any of its parts or accessories, shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than fifteen years or by imprisonment in a jail or house of correction for not more than two and one-half years or by a fine of not more than fifteen thousand dollars, or by both such fine and imprisonment.

Evidence that an identifying number or numbers of a motor vehicle or trailer or part thereof has been intentionally and removed, defaced, altered, changed, destroyed, obliterated, or mutilated, shall be prima facie evidence that the defendant knew or had reason to know that the motor vehicle, or trailer or part thereof had been stolen.

A prosecution commenced under this subdivision shall not be placed on file or continued without a finding and the sentence imposed upon a person convicted of violating this subdivision for a second or subsequent offense shall not be reduced to less than one year imprisonment, nor shall any sentence imposed upon any person be suspended, or reduced, until such person shall have served one year of such sentence if convicted of a second or subsequent such offense.

A person convicted of a second or subsequent offense of violating the provisions of this subdivision shall not be eligible for probation, parole, furlough or work release; provided, however that the commissioner of correction may, on the recommendation of warden, superintendent, or other person in charge of a correctional institution, or the administrator of a county correctional institution, grant to said offender a temporary release in the custody of an officer of such institution for the following purposes: to attend the funeral of next of kin or spouse; to visit a critically ill close relative or spouse; or to obtain emergency medical services unavailable at said institution.

(b) Whoever conceals any motor vehicle or trailer thief knowing him to be such, shall be punished by imprisonment for not more than ten years or by imprisonment in jail or house of correction for not more than two and one-half years or by a fine of not more than five thousand dollars, or both.

(c) A conviction of a violation of this section or any adjudication that a person is a delinquent child by reason thereof, except for a conviction or adjudication for malicious damage to a motor vehicle or trailer, shall be reported forthwith by the court or magistrate to the registrar of motor vehicles who shall revoke immediately the license to operate motor vehicles or the right to operate motor vehicles of the person so convicted or adjudged, and no appeal, motion for new trial or exceptions shall operate to stay the revocation of such license or right to operate. The registrar of motor vehicles after having revoked the license or right to operate of any such person so convicted or adjudged shall issue a new license or reinstate such right to operate, if the prosecution of such person is finally terminated in his favor; otherwise, no new license shall be issued nor shall such right to operate be reinstated until one year after the date of revocation following his original conviction or adjudication if for a first offense, or until five years after the date of revocation following any subsequent conviction or adjudication.

5 crimes in this section

  1. (a) Steals a motor vehicle or trailer

    Penalty
    • Up to 15 years in state prison, or up to 2½ years in a jail or house of correction, or a fine of up to $15,000, or both a fine and imprisonment
    • If second or subsequent offense: Not less than 1 year imprisonment; sentence not suspended or reduced until 1 year is served; not eligible for probation, parole, furlough or work releasemandatory minimumno probationno parole

  2. (a) Maliciously damages a motor vehicle or trailer

    Intent
    • Maliciously
    Penalty
    • Up to 15 years in state prison, or up to 2½ years in a jail or house of correction, or a fine of up to $15,000, or both a fine and imprisonment
    • If second or subsequent offense: Not less than 1 year imprisonment; sentence not suspended or reduced until 1 year is served; not eligible for probation, parole, furlough or work releasemandatory minimumno probationno parole
    Other consequences
    • Prosecution may not be placed on file or continued without a finding for a second or subsequent offense
    • Temporary release in an officer's custody may be granted by the commissioner of correction for a next-of-kin or spouse funeral, a critically ill close relative or spouse, or emergency medical services
    • Report to the registrar of motor vehicles and license revocation do not apply to a conviction or adjudication for malicious damage

  3. (a) Buys, receives, possesses, conceals, or obtains control of a motor vehicle or trailer

    Ways to commit
    • buys · receives · possesses · conceals · obtains control of
    Knowledge
    • Knowing or having reason to know the vehicle or trailer to have been stolen
    Conditions
    • The motor vehicle or trailer was stolen
    • Intentional and malicious removal, defacing or alteration of an identifying number is prima facie evidence of knowledge
    Penalty
    • Up to 15 years in state prison, or up to 2½ years in a jail or house of correction, or a fine of up to $15,000, or both a fine and imprisonment
    • If second or subsequent offense: Not less than 1 year imprisonment; sentence not suspended or reduced until 1 year is served; not eligible for probation, parole, furlough or work releasemandatory minimumno probationno parole

  4. (a) Takes a motor vehicle without the authority of the owner and steals from it any of its parts or accessories

    Penalty
    • Up to 15 years in state prison, or up to 2½ years in a jail or house of correction, or a fine of up to $15,000, or both a fine and imprisonment
    • If second or subsequent offense: Not less than 1 year imprisonment; sentence not suspended or reduced until 1 year is served; not eligible for probation, parole, furlough or work releasemandatory minimumno probationno parole

  5. (b) Conceals any motor vehicle or trailer thief

    Knowledge
    • Knowing him to be such
    Penalty
    • Up to 10 years of imprisonment, or up to 2½ years in a jail or house of correction, or a fine of up to $5,000, or both
Applies to all 5 crimes above
Other consequences
  • Prosecution may not be placed on file or continued without a finding for a second or subsequent offense
  • Court or magistrate reports a conviction or delinquency adjudication to the registrar of motor vehicles, who revokes the license or right to operate immediately; no appeal, motion for new trial or exceptions stays the revocation
  • Revocation lasts until 1 year after revocation for a first offense, or 5 years after revocation for a subsequent conviction or adjudication, unless the prosecution ends in the person's favor
  • Temporary release in an officer's custody may be granted by the commissioner of correction for a next-of-kin or spouse funeral, a critically ill close relative or spouse, or emergency medical services

Simplified explanation

Stealing, maliciously damaging, receiving or concealing a stolen motor vehicle or trailer is punishable by up to 15 years in state prison; concealing a vehicle thief carries up to 10 years.
