c. 266, § 23

Embezzlement of property at fire; effect

See penalty details Property Crimes Ch. 266 · Crimes Against Property

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever steals, conveys away or conceals any furniture, goods, chattels, merchandise or effects of persons whose houses or buildings are on fire or are endangered thereby, and does not, within two days thereafter, restore the same or give notice of his possession thereof to the owner, if known, or, if unknown, to the mayor or one of the aldermen, selectmen or firewards of the place, shall be guilty of larceny.

1 crime in this section

  1. Steals, conveys away or conceals any furniture, goods, chattels, merchandise or effects of persons whose houses or buildings are on fire or are endangered thereby, and does not, within two days thereafter, restore the same or give notice of his possession

    Ways to commit
    • steals · conveys away · conceals
    Conditions
    • The owner's house or building is on fire or endangered thereby
    • Within two days he does not restore the property or give notice of his possession to the owner, if known, or, if unknown, to the mayor or one of the aldermen, selectmen or firewards of the place
    Penalty
    • Guilty of larceny; the penalty is not set in this section

Simplified explanation

Stealing, carrying away or hiding belongings of people whose buildings are on fire or endangered, and not returning them or giving notice within two days, is larceny; the penalty is not set in this section.
