c. 268, § 34

Disguises to obstruct execution of law, performance of duties, or exercise of rights

Up to 1 year Courts & Justice Ch. 268 · Crimes Against Public Justice

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

Whoever disguises himself with intent to obstruct the due execution of the law, or to intimidate, hinder or interrupt an officer or other person in the lawful performance of his duty, or in the exercise of his rights under the constitution or laws of the commonwealth, whether such intent is effected or not, shall be punished by a fine of not more than five hundred dollars or by imprisonment for not more than one year and may if imprisoned also be bound to good behavior for one year after the expiration of such imprisonment.

1 crime in this section

  1. Disguises himself

    Intent
    • With intent to obstruct the due execution of the law, or to intimidate, hinder or interrupt an officer or other person in the lawful performance of his duty, or in the exercise of his rights under the constitution or laws of the commonwealth
    Conditions
    • It does not matter whether such intent is effected or not
    Penalty
    • A fine of up to $500 or imprisonment for up to 1 year
    Other consequences
    • If imprisoned, may also be bound to good behavior for one year after the expiration of the imprisonment

Simplified explanation

Wearing a disguise with intent to obstruct the law or to intimidate or hinder an officer or person in lawful duties or rights is punishable by a fine up to $500 or up to one year in prison.
