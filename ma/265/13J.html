c. 265, § 13J

Assault and battery upon a child; penalties

Up to 15 years Family & Children Ch. 265 · Crimes Against the Person

Official text prohibited actknowledgeintentif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) For the purposes of this section, the following words shall, unless the context indicates otherwise, have the following meanings:—

“”, substantial impairment of the physical condition including any burn, fracture of any bone, subdural hematoma, injury to any internal organ, any injury which occurs as the result of repeated harm to any bodily function or organ including human skin or any physical condition which substantially imperils a child's health or welfare.

“Child”, any person under fourteen years of age.

“Person having care and custody”, a parent, guardian, employee of a home or institution or any other person with equivalent supervision or care of a child, whether the supervision is temporary or permanent.

“Substantial ”, which creates a permanent disfigurement, protracted loss or impairment of a function of a body member, limb or organ, or substantial risk of death.

(b) Whoever commits an assault and battery upon a child and by such assault and battery causes shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than five years or imprisonment in the house of correction for not more than two and one-half years.

Whoever commits an assault and battery upon a child and by such assault and battery causes substantial shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than fifteen years or imprisonment in the house of correction for not more than two and one-half years.

Whoever, having care and custody of a child, wantonly or permits to such child or permits another to commit an assault and battery upon such child, which assault and battery causes , shall be punished by imprisonment for not more than two and one-half years in the .

Whoever, having care and custody of a child, permits substantial to such child or permits another to commit an assault and battery upon such child, which assault and battery causes substantial , shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than five years, or by imprisonment in a jail or house of correction for not more than two and one-half years.

Key terms
  • "Bodily injury": substantial impairment of the physical condition including any burn, fracture of any bone, subdural hematoma, injury to any internal organ, any injury which occurs as the result of repeated harm to any bodily function or organ including human skin or any physical condition which substantially imperils a child's health or welfare
  • "Child": any person under fourteen years of age
  • "Person having care and custody": a parent, guardian, employee of a home or institution or any other person with equivalent supervision or care of a child, whether the supervision is temporary or permanent
  • "Substantial bodily injury": bodily injury which creates a permanent disfigurement, protracted loss or impairment of a function of a body member, limb or organ, or substantial risk of death

Simplified explanation

Assault and battery on a child under 14 causing bodily injury carries up to 5 years in state prison (15 for substantial injury); a caretaker who wantonly or recklessly permits such injury also faces prison.
