c. 265, § 26C

Definition of ''entice''; enticement of child under age 16; punishment

Up to 5 years Family & Children Ch. 265 · Crimes Against the Person

Official text prohibited actmental stateif conditionpenaltylegal term

(a) As used in this section, the term “entice” shall mean to lure, induce, persuade, tempt, incite, solicit, coax or invite.

(b) Any one who entices a child under the age of 16, or someone he believes to be a child under the age of 16, to enter, exit or remain within any vehicle, dwelling, building, or other outdoor space with the intent that he or another person will violate section 13B, 13B½, 13B¾, 13F, 13H, 22, 22A, 22B, 22C, 23, 23A, 23B, 24 or 24B of chapter 265, section 4A, 16, 28, 29, 29A, 29B, 29C, 35A, 53 or 53A of chapter 272, or any offense that has as an element the use or attempted use of force, shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than 5 years, or in the house of correction for not more than 2½ years, or by both imprisonment and a fine of not more than $5,000.

Simplified explanation

Enticing a child under 16 (or someone believed to be) into a vehicle, building, or other space intending a listed sex or violent crime is punishable by up to 5 years in state prison.
